About

I like problems where the answer depends on the odds. As an electrical engineer, that meant sizing a power system under uncertain demand. Now it means statistical models. At the bridge table, it's deciding how to play a hand from incomplete information.

I grew up in Johannesburg and came to the US for a BS in Electrical Engineering at Drexel. I then spent two years as an electrical engineer at Burns Engineering, working on power, lighting, and electrical safety projects for ports, transit agencies, highways, and universities.

Now I'm doing an MS in Data Science at Columbia and working as a research assistant on Bayesian models in the Movement Recovery Lab. My long-term career aspiration is to work in quantitative finance.

Columbia University · Movement Recovery Laboratory

Research Assistant

Bayesian modeling of non-sigmoidal neural recruitment curves. When a nerve pathway is stimulated harder and harder, the muscle's response grows along a "recruitment curve." Models usually assume an S shape, but real data often doesn't follow one. I work on hierarchical Bayesian models in the lab's hbMEP framework that fit more flexible curves and show how much confidence each estimate deserves.

Ports · EV charging#0001/05

Port of Oakland Green Power Microgrid

With Moffatt & Nichol

The project started as a full seaport microgrid: a 1 MW solar array, 6.5 MW of battery storage, and about 150 heavy-duty EV chargers, backed by a $41.6M state grant. I sized and modeled the system, worked on Burns' 30-charger share of the design, and helped coordinate the 30/60/90% reviews.

In later iterations the Port narrowed the scope to EV charging alone. That package went out to bid in December 2025 as 115 heavy-duty chargers across five sites.
